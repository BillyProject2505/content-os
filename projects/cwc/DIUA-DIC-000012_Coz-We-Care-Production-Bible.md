**Validation Checklist:** No duplicated knowledge · Canonical consistency maintained · Traceability preserved.

**6.6.1 Social Caption & Hashtag Standard** — Governs hashtag composition in CWC social-media publication captions as a subordinate rule of Writing Standards.

**6.9.1 Outreach Worker Service-Navigation CTA Standard — OWNER DIRECTIVE, v2.19**

Purpose: make the outreach-worker function visible as a legitimate community access point without blurring the boundary between community outreach and clinical care.

Principles: OWS-P01 Service Visibility · OWS-P02 Community Navigation · OWS-P03 Role Clarity · OWS-P04 Clinical Boundary · OWS-P05 Contextual Relevance.

Rules:

* **ES-031A** CWC educational content SHOULD include a secondary service-navigation CTA that makes the outreach-worker role visible when contextually relevant and when doing so does not displace the artefact's primary message.
* **ES-031B** Priority SHOULD be given to the outreach worker managing the CWC account as the visible community entry point, using role-based wording such as **“petugas penjangkau yang mengelola akun ini”** rather than requiring a personal name.
* **ES-031C** Where truthful to the actual programme role, the outreach worker MAY be described as helping with understandable information, identifying suitable services, referral/linkage, accompaniment/navigation, and community follow-up.
* **ES-031D** Outreach-worker promotion SHALL NOT imply clinical authority. HIV testing and interpretation, clinical assessment, PrEP/PEP eligibility or regimen decisions, diagnosis, prescribing, and treatment decisions remain with qualified health workers / health services.
* **ES-031E** In time-sensitive or safety-sensitive content, the urgent health-service action remains the primary CTA. Outreach-worker navigation is secondary and SHALL NOT delay access to clinical care.
* **ES-031F** CWC SHALL NOT make unsupported claims about the outreach service, including 24/7 availability, guaranteed access, guaranteed confidentiality, free service, or guaranteed outcomes, unless separately verified for the specific context.
* **ES-031G** The default promotion channel MAY be the caption, closing frame/slide, Story CTA, or other context-appropriate service-navigation surface. A separate visual revision is not required when a compliant caption can carry the rule without weakening the educational artefact.

Implementation Example: ✔ “Butuh bantuan mencari layanan atau memahami langkah berikutnya? Petugas penjangkau yang mengelola akun ini dapat membantu memberi informasi, menghubungkan ke layanan, dan mendampingi proses akses. Untuk tes, penilaian klinis, serta keputusan terkait PrEP/PEP, petugas kesehatan tetap menjadi rujukan klinis.”

Non-example: ✘ Wording that presents the outreach worker as the person who decides PrEP/PEP eligibility, interprets test results clinically, prescribes treatment, or guarantees access/outcomes.

Checklist: Outreach role visible when relevant · Primary message preserved · Navigation/support functions stated truthfully · Clinical boundary explicit · No unsupported service claim · Urgent clinical CTA retains priority when applicable.

**6.10 Editorial Validation Standard** — Editorial Validation evaluates compliance with the Editorial System; validates editorial production knowledge only.

Principles: EVS-P01 Standards-Based Validation · EVS-P02 Objective Evaluation · EVS-P03 Complete Editorial Coverage · EVS-P04 Independent Validation.

Implementation Examples: ✔ Validation confirms compliance with applicable standards. ✘ Validation relies on personal writing preference.

**7.3 Communication Artefact System** — Governs every communication artefact produced across CWC. Canonical Decision: single canonical source for Communication Artefacts; every Artefact SHALL translate canonical production decisions into communication outputs without redefining them.

Production Intent (every Artefact SHALL): communicate the intended message clearly · preserve canonical identity · support the intended communication objective · remain appropriate to context; prioritize communication before presentation.

Implementation Freedom: AI MAY determine Artefact format/structure/implementation provided Canonical Identity is preserved, Production Intent is achieved, and upstream decisions remain unchanged.

Domain Boundary: SHALL NOT determine Editorial Strategy, Visual Identity, Production Workflow, or Publication Workflow.

AI Production Boundary: AI inherits all applicable canonical decisions before producing any Artefact; Artefacts realize, not redefine, canonical decisions.

**7.4 Visual Specification** — Governs the canonical visual systems used to realize Communication Artefacts. Canonical Decision: single canonical source for visual implementation; establishes six canonical visual systems (§7.4.1–§7.4.6); implements, does not redefine, upstream decisions.

Production Intent (VDS SHALL, applies to all six systems below): preserve canonical visual identity inherited per §7.2 · establish consistent visual implementation · support reproducible visual production · ensure visual consistency across all Artefacts; prioritize communication before visual expression.

**Shared Domain Boundary (applies to §7.4.1–§7.4.6 below; stated once per UPR-002 One Concept, One Home, not repeated in each sub-system's own Domain Boundary):** none of the six visual systems below SHALL determine Foundation (Chapter 1); Brand Strategy or Brand Identity (owned by the Brand System, Chapter 2, per §2.6); Audience Strategy (Chapter 3); Content Strategy (Chapter 4); Content Architecture (Chapter 5); Editorial Strategy (Chapter 6); Production Workflow; Publication Workflow; or concrete visual-asset identity, specification, or reference (e.g. logo, official brand colors, footer platform) — owned by the Canonical Asset System ecosystem (§4 Exclusions, [BUS-19](https://linear.app/bussiness-content-os/issue/BUS-19/coz-we-care-canonical-asset-system-cas)). Each sub-system's own Domain Boundary below states only its distinctions from the other five visual systems.

**\[DECISION\]** Color is not established as a seventh visual sub-system alongside Canvas, Layout, Surface, Typography, Graphic Elements, and Imagery (§7.4.1–§7.4.6). Concrete color specification (e.g. official brand colors) is a concrete visual-asset spec, not a conceptual visual-translation principle, and is therefore owned by the Canonical Asset System ecosystem (§4 Exclusions, [BUS-19](https://linear.app/bussiness-content-os/issue/BUS-19/coz-we-care-canonical-asset-system-cas)) — consistent with how logo and footer-platform specs are already excluded above. Where any of the six systems above needs to reference color (e.g. the Surface System's background treatment, or the Graphic Elements System's visual signaling), it inherits the concrete color values from the Canonical Asset System ecosystem rather than defining them — the six systems govern how color is applied structurally, never what the color values themselves are.

**Shared AI Production Boundary (applies to §7.4.1–§7.4.6 below):** AI inherits Canonical Identity (§7.2) and all applicable canonical decisions before applying any visual specification; the six systems below are applied in the order presented (Canvas, then Layout, then Surface, Typography, Graphic Elements, and Imagery), each building on the decisions already fixed by the ones before it, not simultaneously or in arbitrary order.

**7.4.1 Canvas System** — Defines the rendering foundation of every Artefact: its base technical/format properties (dimensions, orientation, aspect ratio, resolution, base render conditions). Canonical Decision: single canonical source for an Artefact's rendering-field technical properties; SHALL translate, not redefine, upstream decisions.

Production Intent (Canvas System SHALL): establish the rendering field's technical properties for every Artefact · preserve format consistency across Artefacts of the same type · maintain traceability to the Communication Artefact's intended output context (§7.3) · support reproducible rendering; prioritize technical correctness before visual treatment.

Implementation Freedom: AI MAY determine specific canvas dimensions/format within a given Artefact type, provided established format conventions for that Artefact type are preserved and Production Intent is achieved. Implementation techniques are never Canonical Decisions.

Domain Boundary: Canvas System SHALL NOT determine how elements are spatially arranged within the canvas (Layout System, §7.4.2); SHALL NOT determine the visual treatment of supporting background/container surfaces (Surface System, §7.4.3) — Canvas governs the rendering field's technical properties only, never its visual background treatment; SHALL NOT determine typography, graphic elements, or imagery treatment (§7.4.4–§7.4.6).

AI Production Boundary: AI establishes canvas technical properties before any layout, surface, typographic, graphic, or imagery decision is applied within it.

**7.4.2 Layout System** — Defines the spatial organization of visual elements within the canvas: placement, alignment, grouping, and visual hierarchy. Canonical Decision: single canonical source for spatial organization within an Artefact's canvas; SHALL translate, not redefine, upstream decisions.

Production Intent (Layout System SHALL): organize visual elements within the canvas's established technical properties · preserve spatial consistency across Artefacts of the same type · support visual hierarchy aligned with the Communication Artefact's intended message (§7.3) · maintain reproducible spatial arrangement; prioritize clarity of organization before decorative arrangement.

Implementation Freedom: AI MAY determine specific spatial arrangement within an Artefact, provided canvas technical properties (§7.4.1) remain unchanged and Production Intent is achieved.

Domain Boundary: Layout System SHALL NOT determine the canvas's own technical/rendering properties (Canvas System, §7.4.1); SHALL NOT determine the visual treatment of supporting surfaces themselves (Surface System, §7.4.3) — Layout governs where elements sit, not what a surface looks like; SHALL NOT determine typography, graphic elements, or imagery treatment (§7.4.4–§7.4.6); SHALL NOT determine the structural organization of content itself (Content Architecture, Chapter 5) — Layout organizes the visual placement of already-structured content, it does not restructure content.

AI Production Boundary: AI applies spatial organization only after canvas properties are established and content structure (Chapter 5) and Communication Artefact type (§7.3) are known.

**7.4.4 Typography System** — Defines the visual treatment of written communication: hierarchy, emphasis, readability, and legibility. Canonical Decision: single canonical source for the visual expression of written content; SHALL translate, not redefine, upstream decisions.

Production Intent (Typography System SHALL): translate written content into legible, hierarchy-appropriate visual form · preserve typographic consistency across Artefacts of the same type · support the Editorial System's (Chapter 6) intended emphasis and tone visually · maintain reproducible typographic treatment; prioritize legibility before decorative type treatment.

Global Typeface Decision — OWNER-APPROVED ([BUS-64](https://linear.app/bussiness-content-os/issue/BUS-64/cwc-typography-system-global-typeface-and-hierarchy-definition), 2026-09-17)

* Primary Typeface: **Montserrat**.
* Approved default production weights: **Regular 400, Medium 500, SemiBold 600, Bold 700, ExtraBold 800**.
* Weights 100–300 and 900 are outside the default production set and require an explicit governed format/content exception before final publication use.
* No global Secondary Typeface is established at baseline.
* No global handwritten/accent Typeface is established at baseline. Expressive or handwritten lettering may be introduced only through separately governed format/series treatment and SHALL NOT silently become a global CWC typeface.

**Semantic Role Hierarchy**

| Role | Default Weight | Global Function |
| -- | -- | -- |
| Hero / Display Headline | 800 | Highest visual emphasis |
| Headline | 700–800 | Primary content title |
| Subheadline | 700 | Secondary title / explanatory hierarchy |
| Kicker / Eyebrow / Label | 600 | Short categorical or navigational text |
| Body | 400–500 | Main reading text; prioritize legibility |
| Supporting Text | 500 | Compact explanatory/supporting copy |
| CTA | 700 | Action emphasis |
| Data / Key Number | 700–800 | Quantitative emphasis |

**Case Principle**

* Sentence case is the default for body and most explanatory text.
* Headline case may follow editorial intent; full uppercase is not the default headline treatment.
* ALL CAPS is reserved for short kickers, labels, or controlled emphasis where legibility remains acceptable.

**Fallback Principle**

A neutral system sans-serif MAY be used only as a temporary draft/proof fallback when Montserrat is technically unavailable. Final publication output requiring typography fidelity MUST use Montserrat or be corrected during deterministic/manual post-production. Fallback rendering SHALL NOT be treated as canonical visual fidelity.

**Numeric / Utility Typography**

No separate utility/data typeface is established. Data, numerals, and key figures use Montserrat within the approved weight hierarchy unless a future governed decision establishes a dedicated solution.

**Authority Split — One Concept, One Home**

* CPB §7.4.4 owns global Typeface family, approved weight set, semantic role hierarchy, global case principles, fallback policy, secondary/accent policy, and global numeric/data typography principle.
* FSS owns format-specific implementation constraints including exact font sizes, line-height/leading, format-dependent tracking/letter spacing, minimum readable size by canvas/format, and format-specific scaling/adaptation.
* CAB/CRS own typography intrinsic to canonical assets. Asset-specific locked typography is not overridden by the global rule.
* Existing AS-004 CWC Official Footer Platform remains protected under its existing CAB/CRS authority. Its exact locked typography values are defined by the current authoritative CAB/CRS representation and are not duplicated here.

Implementation Freedom: AI MAY determine specific typographic treatment within an Artefact only within the global rules above and applicable FSS constraints, provided canvas, layout, and surface decisions (§7.4.1–§7.4.3) remain unchanged and Production Intent is achieved.

Domain Boundary: Typography System SHALL NOT determine the meaning, wording, or editorial intent of written content (owned by the Editorial System, Chapter 6) — Typography governs the visual expression of already-written content, never its authorship; SHALL NOT determine canvas, layout, or surface properties (§7.4.1–§7.4.3); SHALL NOT determine graphic elements or imagery treatment (§7.4.5–§7.4.6).

AI Production Boundary: AI applies typographic treatment only after editorial content (Chapter 6) exists and canvas/layout/surface decisions are established.

**7.4.5 Graphic Elements System** — Defines non-imagery supporting graphic elements: ornamentation, dividers, functional marks, and visual signaling elements. Canonical Decision: single canonical source for supporting, non-representational graphic elements; SHALL translate, not redefine, upstream decisions.

AI Production Boundary: AI applies supporting graphic elements only after canvas, layout, surface, and typography decisions are established.

**7.4.6 Imagery System** — Defines the visual representation of subjects — people, objects, environments — within an Artefact. Canonical Decision: single canonical source for the visual representation of real subjects; SHALL translate, not redefine, upstream decisions.

Production Intent (Imagery System SHALL): represent subjects visually in a manner consistent with the Communication Artefact's intended message and audience (§7.3, Chapter 3) · preserve imagery-treatment consistency across Artefacts of the same type · avoid imagery that stigmatizes or misrepresents subjects, consistent with Core Production Principles (Chapter 1, CP-005 Non-Stigmatizing Communication) · maintain reproducible imagery treatment; prioritize accurate, respectful representation before decorative imagery.

Implementation Freedom: AI MAY determine specific imagery treatment within an Artefact, provided canvas, layout, surface, typography, and graphic-element decisions (§7.4.1–§7.4.5) remain unchanged and Production Intent is achieved.

Domain Boundary: Imagery System SHALL NOT determine non-representational supporting graphics (owned by Graphic Elements System, §7.4.5); SHALL NOT determine canvas, layout, surface, or typography properties (§7.4.1–§7.4.4); SHALL NOT determine concrete brand-asset imagery specifications (e.g. the canonical Brand Presenter's locked visual identity) — owned by the Canonical Asset System ecosystem (§4 Exclusions).

AI Production Boundary: AI applies imagery treatment only after canvas, layout, surface, typography, and graphic-element decisions are established, and only in a manner consistent with Core Production Principles (Chapter 1).
